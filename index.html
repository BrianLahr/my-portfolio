About Me

I am an Electrical Engineer from the São Carlos School of Engineering, University of São Paulo (EESC-USP), with an emphasis in Electronics, and I am currently pursuing an MSc in Electrical Engineering at the same institution.

My current research investigates efficient information exchange between two hearing devices for distributed binaural speech enhancement. I am interested in what should be communicated between the ears when bandwidth, latency, memory, and power are limited, connecting multichannel signal processing, machine learning, quantization, and hearing technology.

My earlier work spans physics-informed neural networks, RF and microwave sensing, research software, and FAPESP-funded industrial R&D. This combination has shaped the way I approach research: from mathematical modelling and algorithms to electromagnetic simulation, measurement, data analysis, and experimental validation.

Current Research

Binaural Speech Enhancement

My MSc research studies efficient interaural representations for distributed binaural speech enhancement under communication constraints. The central question is simple: when two hearing devices cannot exchange unlimited data, what information is actually worth transmitting?

The work compares local processing, coded remote audio, engineered low-rate features, processed signals, and learned rate-constrained representations under matched communication budgets, evaluating speech enhancement, spatial fidelity, and effective bitrate.

Speech Enhancement Binaural DSP Machine Learning Quantization Rate Constraints

Tiresias Hearing-Aid Platform

I am involved with Tiresias, an open-source hearing-aid research platform developed at USP for real-time audio processing. It provides a practical path for testing algorithms under the latency, computational, memory, and power constraints that matter in wearable hearing devices.

My longer-term goal is to connect algorithmic research in binaural enhancement and efficient learned representations with realistic embedded evaluation on hearing-device hardware.

Selected Projects

BACHELOR'S THESIS · 2026

Microwave Sensor for Fertilizer Quantification in Water

Optimized, fabricated, and experimentally characterized a microstrip hairpin-resonator sensor for quantifying urea and potassium chloride (KCl) dissolved in water. The geometry was optimized in Ansys HFSS using a figure of merit combining resonance-frequency shift, loaded quality factor, and electric-field interaction with the sample. The fabricated sensor was characterized with a vector network analyzer and tested from 6.25 to 200 ppm. Measurable concentration-dependent responses were obtained for both analytes, supporting the design as a proof of concept while also revealing important repeatability and sample-positioning limitations.
